Panel meter study

Visual companion to PANEL-METER-STUDY.md. A scope is watched. A multimeter is read. A panel meter is glanced at. Every needle below is a real simulated second-order system — the physics runs live.

Finding 01 · the headline

Ballistics — a needle does not show you the signal

It shows you the signal through a defined mechanical response, and that response is standardised because the raw signal is unreadable. All three meters below are fed the identical live power signal at the same instant.

RAW. Every sample, no filtering. Technically the most current data on screen — and completely unreadable.

VU — 300 ms, symmetric. Average-responding, minor overshoot. Calm and musical, but transients are smoothed away entirely.

PPM — 5 ms attack, ~1.7 s decay. Deliberately asymmetric: catches the peak instantly, then releases slowly enough to actually read it.

This is the answer to PwrMon's jittery readout. Not "smooth it more" (loses the spike) and not "show raw" (unreadable). A 90 W spike lasting 200 ms should jump instantly to full height and then ease down over a second or two — visible, readable, and honest about the peak. If PwrMon adopts one thing from all three studies, it is this.

Finding 02

Damping — there is exactly one correct amount

A moving-coil movement is a coil on a spring: a mechanical resonator. Real meters add eddy-current damping so the pointer settles quickly without oscillating. Below, the same repeating step input through three damping ratios.

ζ = 0.25 — underdamped. Overshoots and rings. Jittery and exhausting; you wait for it to settle before trusting it.

ζ = 1.0 — critically damped. Fastest possible approach with no overshoot. The target.

ζ = 3.0 — overdamped. Never overshoots but crawls. Reads as laggy and unresponsive.

Any animated readout in PwrMon — needle, bar, or number — should be a critically damped second-order response, not a linear tween and not a naive EMA. It is barely more code and it is the whole difference between "animated" and "instrument".

Finding 03

Zones before numbers, and a needle with a memory

Coloured bands let you read state before value — a glance answers "is this OK?" without processing a number at all. This is the highest-bandwidth thing a panel meter does, and at 16 px in a system tray it is the only channel that works. The faint trailing pointer is a peak-hold ghost: dragged along and left at the maximum so the peak stays visible after the main needle falls back.

Live, with derived zones and peak-hold. Green = idle band around the learned baseline · amber = active · red = heavy draw. Ghost pointer holds session peak; zones could derive from the learned baseline per machine rather than being configured.

Finding 04

Three studies, one conclusion

Researched independently, the three instrument families agree — which is about as strong a signal as design research ever gives you. These are not aesthetic preferences; they are the correct answers to problems every measuring instrument has.
