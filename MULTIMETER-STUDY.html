Multimeter study

Visual companion to MULTIMETER-STUDY.md. The scope study was about the trace. This one is about the number — and whether we have earned the digits we print.

Finding 01

Have we earned that decimal point?

PwrMon prints +80.4 W. But this machine's fuel gauge reports quantized values and refreshes only every ~15–30 s. A multimeter would never print a tenths digit off that data. Below: identical underlying readings, presented three ways.

80.4W
DCHOLDMIN MAX

TODAY — overstated. Tenths digit implies 0.1 W resolution we do not have. It is noise rendered as precision.

80W
DCHOLDMIN MAX

HONEST — truncated. Shows only what the source can support. Calm, and never lies.

80.4W
DCSTALEMIN MAX

MARKED — best of both. Keeps the digit but de-emphasises it, plus a staleness annunciator. Precision shown as uncertain.

Cheapest high-integrity change available. Derive displayed precision from the source's real resolution instead of from double formatting. Measurable today: log raw WMI values, find the smallest non-zero delta.

Finding 02

Why every good DMM has a bargraph

Digits are excellent for precision and terrible for movement. A value flickering between 80.4 and 79.1 is unreadable — your eye cannot integrate it. The bargraph updates faster than the digits and carries direction, rate, and instability. Both panels below are fed the identical live signal.

—W
DCAUTO

WRONG — digits at full sample rate. Technically the most current data on screen, and practically unreadable.

—W
0255075100 W
DCAUTO

RIGHT — digits slow, bar fast. Number is readable; the bar carries the behaviour at full rate. Nothing is lost.

Finding 03

Accuracy is two terms — and the second is the honest one

DMM accuracy reads ±(% of reading + N counts). The percentage is gain error that scales with the value; the + counts term is a fixed floor of uncertainty in the last digits, published on the instrument. It is a formal admission that the final digits are noise.

±(2% + 2 counts) across range. Note the band never collapses to zero: at low readings the fixed counts term dominates entirely.

Finding 04

The mirror strip — an instrument admitting its own error

The Simpson 260 carries a mirrored band along its scale. You align the needle with its own reflection before reading, guaranteeing your eye is perpendicular and removing parallax. The instrument ships a physical correction for its own reading error — it does not assume the user is infallible.

Stacked arcs + mirror band. Multiple scales share one needle; the ohms arc runs backwards and non-linear because the physics is reciprocal. Scales get shaped to the measurement rather than forced uniform.

Finding 05

The modes exist because you are not watching

Nearly every DMM button is about not having to stare at the meter. For an always-on background monitor, that is not a nice-to-have — it is the entire premise.
