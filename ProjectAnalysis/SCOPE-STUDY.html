Oscilloscope study

Visual companion to SCOPE-STUDY.md. Every trace below is rendered live in canvas from simulated power data — the phosphor behaviour and beam physics are actually computed, not painted.

Finding 01

Brightness encodes dwell time

On a CRT the beam traces a continuous path at constant speed along its own arc. Where the signal is flat the beam covers little ground and dwells, exciting the phosphor hard — the line is bright. Where the signal jumps, the same energy smears over a much longer path — the line goes dim. Trace brightness varies inversely with slope, and almost every "retro scope" UI misses it.

WRONG — uniform stroke. Every segment identical. The 90 W spike shouts as loudly as an hour of idle.

RIGHT — dwell-weighted. Alpha ∝ 1/segment-length. Steady draw burns in; transients are faint streaks.

Why it matters here: this isn't only authenticity — it's honesty. A 200 ms spike to 90 W contributed almost nothing to consumed energy. It should look fainter than an hour at 15 W. The rendering tells the truth about where time was spent.

Finding 02

Four phosphors, same signal

Phosphor type is a real engineering choice designated by a P-number, trading colour, brightness and persistence. Each panel below renders identical data through a different phosphor's characteristics.

P31

green · <1 ms · the canon

Near peak eye response, high burn resistance. Most analog scopes from the '70s on. Reads instantly as "oscilloscope".

P7 cascade

blue → amber · ~60 s · radar

Two physical layers: blue flash where the beam is, yellow-green glow underneath decaying for a minute. Recency becomes colour.

P11

blue · short · photographic

Built for photographing traces onto film. Cooler and more clinical than P31 — reads as laboratory rather than workshop.

Storage

amber · bistable · written stays

Bistable storage tube: flatter, dimmer, and everything written stays written. The natural partner to infinite persistence.

Finding 03

P7 encodes recency for free

The standout. Because P7 is a genuine two-layer cascade, the live edge burns blue-white and the past decays through yellow-green over roughly a minute. On a live power chart that means now is unmistakable without a legend, a marker, or a label — the physics does the work. Below: the same trace at 1× and 4× width so the gradient is legible.

P7 cascade, live edge at right. Blue-white flash → amber-green decay. Dwell weighting applied simultaneously, so flat idle regions still burn brighter than transients within the same age band.

Finding 04

Graticule discipline

Scope graticules are 8 × 10 major divisions, and the fine 5-way subdivision ticks appear only along the centre horizontal and vertical axes — never across the whole field. That single restraint is why a real graticule reads calm while a naive uniform grid reads busy.

Naive. Uniform fine grid everywhere. The grid competes with the data.

Scope convention. 8×10 majors, minor ticks on centre axes only, zero line emphasised. The grid recedes; the trace dominates.

Finding 05

WATTS/DIV — the functional win

PwrMon currently refits the Y axis to visible data on every sample, so the same wattage drifts to a different screen height second to second and the labels never settle. A scope would never do this: it steps in a detented 1-2-5 ladder and holds until you deliberately click it. Both panels show identical data.

Auto-fit (today). Axis rescales continuously — heights aren't comparable across time, labels land on arbitrary values.

20 W/DIV, detented. Round numbers, stable baseline, grid usable as a ruler. Steps only when the trace nears the edge.

This one is not aesthetics. It's a straight legibility upgrade that happens to also be authentic — the rare case where the homage is the better engineering.

Finding 06

Intensity grading — the envelope of normal

Digital scopes histogram how often the trace crosses each point and map frequency of occurrence to brightness. Applied to 48 hours of power data, the machine's habitual behaviour emerges as a bright band, and genuine anomalies stay visible as faint outliers instead of being averaged into nothing.

Simulated 48 h, intensity-graded. Bright band = where this machine normally lives. The isolated excursion at right is the kind of event that a mean-and-max summary would erase entirely.

Finding 07

Trigger — the feature 2026-07-16 was begging for

Set a level, a slope, and a mode; the instrument watches so you don't have to. LEVEL 60 W · SLOPE ↑ · MODE SINGLE — "freeze and show me the next time draw crosses 60 W." For an app whose founding story is catching an anomaly nobody was watching, this is the opposite of decoration.

Armed at 60 W rising. Dashed line = trigger level. The marker shows the capture point; everything before it is pre-trigger history the instrument had already buffered — so you see what led up to the event, not just the aftermath.
