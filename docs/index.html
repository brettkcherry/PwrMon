PwrMon

Live power telemetry for Windows laptops. Because every battery app shows you health summaries when what you want to know is what your machine is pulling right now, in watts.

Download for Windows Free, no account, no telemetry. Not code-signed yet — SmartScreen will warn on first run; verify the SHA-256 in the release notes.
Released, and tested on exactly one machine.

PwrMon runs daily on its author's machine and does what this page says. What it hasn't had is contact with other hardware: verified on one laptop, and its driverless sensor path is confirmed on Intel only. Every claim here is scoped to what has actually been observed, and will widen as hardware reports arrive.

On battery, discharge rate is your total system draw — that's physics, not an estimate. Windows knows this number. It just won't show it to you, and neither will the battery apps on the Store, which prefer to report a wear percentage and a wildly optimistic time-remaining guess. PwrMon reads the fuel gauge directly and puts the watts on screen.

The design constraint the whole project hangs on: read real silicon power without loading a kernel driver that Microsoft has blocklisted. Most tools in this category ship WinRing0. PwrMon doesn't, and never loads it.

What it does

Live power flow

Charge/discharge watts from the battery's own fuel gauge over ACPI, plus net flow, voltage and current.

CPU & iGPU watts

RAPL package, core and iGPU power — with no administrator and no kernel driver, via Windows' own Energy Meter counters.

History chart

Pan/zoomable multi-series chart over 5 minutes to 48 hours, with AC plug/unplug and resume markers. Persists across restarts.

Temperatures

Drive °C with no admin at all; CPU package, hottest core and throttle headroom in the elevated tier.

Tray wattage ticker

The live number rendered as the tray icon — green charging, orange discharging, red heavy draw. "Heavy" is learned from this machine's own discharge history rather than a fixed wattage, so it means the same thing on a tablet and a gaming laptop.

Floating mini-graph

Borderless translucent sparkline plotting any of eight series over 60 seconds to 24 hours. Draggable, resizable, optionally always-on-top and click-through.

Estimates that aren't garbage

Time-to-empty and time-to-full from 30-second smoothed rates, not Windows' EstimatedRunTime.

Your data stays put

Daily CSV history under %LocalAppData%, pruned on a retention window you set. Exportable. Never uploaded.

What it looks like

Sensor tiers

PwrMon reads CPU and iGPU power from whichever of two sources is available, and prefers the one that asks nothing of you. Battery wattage — the number that tells you your total system draw — works at every tier.

TierWhat you getRequirement
Default
every user		 Battery watts & health, CPU/iGPU watts via Energy Meter counters, CPU load, drive °C Nothing. No UAC prompt, no driver, unaffected by Memory Integrity.
Full
elevated		 Everything above, plus platform (PSys) power, CPU package and hottest-core temperature, throttle headroom Administrator + PawnIO, a signed HVCI-compatible sensor driver

The default tier is the part most tools in this category don't have. Elevation is an upgrade for platform power and temperatures — not a prerequisite for watts.

Where it honestly stands

The interesting parts of a project this age are the edges. These are the real ones, taken from the repo's own ISSUES.md:

  • Verified on one machine — a Zenbook UX3404VA (i7-13700H, Iris Xe). No second machine, no AMD testing.
  • The driverless tier is an Intel claim. The Energy Meter counter path is what makes the no-admin tier work, and it has only been observed on Intel. Until someone runs it on AMD, that's the widest gap between what the docs promise and what's been seen.
  • No iGPU temperature on Intel integrated graphics. Four routes tested and ruled out. Reading it needs a kernel driver PwrMon deliberately doesn't ship — Task Manager doesn't show it either, for the same reason.
  • No battery temperature where the WMI class has no instances, which is the case on the reference machine.
  • The fuel gauge is slower than the app. PwrMon polls up to twice a second, but most gauges republish on their own ~15–30 s cadence. Rather than print a stale tenths-place number as though it were fresh, the readout drops to whole watts and tells you how long the reading has been held.

Privacy & security

  • No telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting. PwrMon reaches the network only when you press a button, and never in the background. Two paths exist: the optional PawnIO download, behind an explicit consent dialog with the installer's signature verified and its signer shown to you first; and the update check under Settings → Updates.
  • Updates are checked only on request, and verified before they run. The release manifest is signed with an ECDSA P-256 key whose public half is compiled into the binary, and it carries the installer's SHA-256 — so one signature authenticates both. A manifest that fails verification is reported as a failure, not swallowed as "no update available." There is no background check and no silent install.
  • No WinRing0 — the driver on Microsoft's vulnerable-driver blocklist that most tools in this category use to read CPU power. Not shipped, never loaded.
  • Elevation is optional and never silent. Battery telemetry needs none.
